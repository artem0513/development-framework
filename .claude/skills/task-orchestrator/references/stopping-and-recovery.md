# Stopping and recovery

Use on a requested stop, repeated failure, unknown external effect, explicit limit or interruption. Apply the persisted policy; do not silently rewrite a saved policy when defaults change.

## Progress-mode recovery

New tasks default to `recovery_mode=progress`, `stagnation_limit=3`, no default stage/task cycle cap, no default total diagnostic-pass cap and no default active-time budget. `repeat_failure_limit=2` remains the fixed/legacy-policy repeat threshold; each diagnostic is bounded to 1200 seconds. Respect explicit configured limits, temporary provider/client retry policies and saved legacy policies, including their fixed cycle/diagnostic caps. Lack of a default total cap is not permission for an endless loop.

Track recovery against the original underlying obligation/problem. Three consecutive unsuccessful cycles without verified progress trigger reassessment, not an automatic total-task ceiling. Progress is a newly satisfied original obligation or verified elimination of an observed failure, supported by fresh executed checks and acceptance/review evidence as required. A different error paragraph, edited bytes, a new hypothesis, temporary success followed by regression, or renaming/decomposing/delegating the same obligation does not create progress. Re-satisfying an oscillating criterion does not manufacture a new allowance.

During discovery, choose checks with useful original-obligation granularity. For traceable verbose unittest cases, an original test check may declare `progress_test_sources`: the complete immutable project-relative test/helper/config boundary. Recovery can then credit each original failure that genuinely passes once, even while the mandatory command still fails. The original case set, test boundary, startup configuration and executed receipt must remain intact; changed, skipped, added, renamed, ambiguous or incomplete cases earn no partial credit. Missing/unsupported boundaries use verified whole checks and acceptance criteria. This accounting does not turn a failing required check into a passing gate. Typed original discriminating probes can establish progress through their declared outcomes; free-form diagnostic claims alone cannot.

At reassessment, inspect the accumulated evidence and identify a discriminating explanation and concrete corrective probe. Persist the diagnostic before launch and keep its outcome and probe history across interruption. Continue only when actual new evidence supports an in-scope next probe and no blocker or explicit limit forbids it. After that probe, require verified progress to resume the normal repair loop. If it produces none, stop automatic mutation and record the unresolved problem and exact next requirement; do not trigger an endless chain of fresh diagnostics from identical evidence. A crash, chat resume or label change cannot reopen a consumed probe. A genuinely changed external state or new discriminating evidence requires explicit reconciliation with the stopped record before another supported probe.

Persist history at the root and associate it with the original problem across aliases, child stages, delegates, contract revisions and resumes. Share spending under any explicit bounds. Monotonic verified progress can support more than three total cycles; it never resets explicit hard budgets or grants new scope. Repair only actionable in-scope findings, then rerun affected checks and separate independent AC/review on current artifacts; avoid gratuitous refactoring.

An unresolved blocker stops affected mutation even when the gate also reports REJECTED. With explicit known stage impact, hold affected stages and dependents while independently approved work continues; absent/ambiguous mappings stay conservative. An explicit resume can probe restored checks/independent verification before repair without resetting history. New material conditions follow questions-and-approval; in-scope technical fixes reuse the approved plan.

When current evidence confirms a DevelopmentFramework defect prevents the approved workflow from advancing, stop the affected path. Save the exact command, its blocking condition, the source or proof already observed, and the framework fix or restored evidence needed to resume. Keep independent approved work moving. Repeating the same error without a discriminating check does not establish the cause. Preserve approved requirements, scenario mappings, and evidence semantics. Resume the affected path when relevant inputs or the framework change. Use private check inputs for executable checks of their declared evidence IDs.

For ordinary in-scope corrections, keep the approved contract and proposal. Apply the correction, refresh affected evidence and obtain a current independent follow-up for the open finding under its existing ID. Use `task plan` for additional authorized checks. Material changes to the approved outcome, architecture, effects or scope go through the owner decision path.

## External effects and stop

Before an external effect, persist a stable action ID and intent. Keep the same logical action ID across retries and recipe edits; intentionally distinct authorized effects need distinct logical IDs. A changed recipe cannot discard known application history. Lost acknowledgement after an attempted operation means UNKNOWN. Observe/reconcile the operation and actual resource before retrying. `visible=false`, an empty eventually consistent listing or an unavailable observation does not prove nonapplication. Resolve UNKNOWN only with proven application or authoritative terminal absence/nonapplication; only a reconciled safe outcome can permit a retry under existing authority. Do not blindly recreate, redeploy or roll back. Keep unresolved operations explicit across task/session boundaries. Recipe edits and renamed actions do not prove a new effect: retain the original approved resource footprint and reconcile unresolved overlapping or unknown footprints before mutation; only known disjoint approved targets may proceed independently.

In the runtime, automated terminal-absence evidence must identify the actual current provider submission captured from the effect command's JSON `provider_attempt_id`, with matching observation identity and `absence_confirmed: true`. Missing or stale submission binding stays UNKNOWN. Reading the same provider response into a new local log does not create new proof; recipe authors must establish authoritative provider status.

Preserve case-sensitive URI paths and opaque resource IDs when establishing identity; ambiguous aliases may block work, but must not prove two resources identical. On stop, stop scheduling changes, settle or record running operations, and use project-context-manager plus finish-and-reports to persist a checkpoint, dated closure, blockers and one next action. A hard process kill preserves only the last completed transaction. Never describe an interrupted session as completed work.
